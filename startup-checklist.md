# Launch a Kyle hotdog trailer, step by step

_Last updated November 15, 2026: added Sabrett as a premium frank option and a battery bank in place of a gas generator._

Aaron's steam-only hotdog trailer can open in Kyle for roughly **$30,000 (low) to $50,000 (mid)** with a battery bank instead of a gas generator, not counting the tow vehicle. The budget sequence is: a new US-built 6x10 shell with a sink package for about **$10,930**, about **$2,000 of plug-in 120V steam and cold-holding equipment**, and roughly **$1,100–$1,500 in state and county permits**. The biggest regulatory change is that **since July 1, 2026, Texas DSHS, not Hays County, issues the health license for mobile food vendors**. A hotdog unit is a **Type II license: $618 application + $400 pre-licensing inspection, due together**, and Aaron cannot sell until he passes that inspection ([DSHS](https://www.dshs.texas.gov/mobile-food-vendors)). Fire stays local: a **$100 annual Hays County Fire Marshal operational permit**, which covers Kyle city limits ([Hays County Fire checklist](https://www.hayscountytx.gov/DocumentCenter/View/4208/Mobile-Food-Vehicles-PDF)). City of Kyle permits are also still needed. Three open questions decide real money and should be settled by phone before buying anything. First, whether reheating commissary-cooked onions on the trailer pushes him to the costlier **Type III** license ($876 + $500). Second, whether the Fire Marshal agrees in writing that a steam-only unit needs **no Type I hood or suppression system**; Buda put that system at about $18,000 ([Community Impact](https://communityimpact.com/san-marcos-buda-kyle/government/buda-city-council-explores-concerns-with-mobile-food-vendor-regulations/)). Third, which commissary will sign his authorization letter. One menu fix is needed too: **Best's Kosher franks no longer exist**. Sara Lee discontinued the brand around February 2009 ([AmazingRibs](https://amazingribs.com/ruminations/2009-02-17-best-hot-dog-gone/)), so the menu should switch to **Hebrew National 6" 8:1 foodservice beef franks (about $0.95 each)** and stop using the Best's name.

**How to read this report.** Items marked **[VERIFY]** come from an older source or a single source, or the researchers could not confirm them. Items marked **[ESTIMATE]** are market-knowledge ranges with no source. Everything else is cited.

## Phase 1: settle six questions by phone before spending money

The 2026 state takeover made older local guidance unreliable. Hays County's former rules (return to the commissary daily, no porta-potties for customers, a $50 trip charge) applied to the county health permit, which no longer counts after July 1, 2026 ([Hays County](https://www.hayscountytx.gov/food-establishments); [DSHS](https://www.dshs.texas.gov/mobile-food-vendors)). State law now bars cities from banning a licensed vendor, setting vending hours by rule, or requiring GPS or "constant motion." Location, zoning and fire codes still apply (HB 2844, §437B.003/.051/.101) ([HB 2844 engrossed](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/pdf/HB02844E.pdf)). The researchers worked from the House-passed text. The enrolled law may differ, as the fee conflict noted below shows.

## Phase 3: trailer purchase, title and registration (weeks 2–8)

Buy a **US-built 6x10 shell with the sink package already plumbed**, then add plug-in equipment. This is the cheapest route to passing inspection. The **Triple A Trailers 6x10 (SKU 17263) is $10,930** and includes:

- a 3-compartment sink with drainboards, plus a separate handwash sink
- **30 gal fresh / 52 gal gray tanks**, which meet the "wastewater at least 15% larger" rule (30 × 1.15 = 34.5)
- a 6 gal water heater and a 120V pump
- a 50A panel with a 25 ft shore cord
- a 40x64" window and a 32" side door
- a **4,400-lb axle with electric brakes and a breakaway kit**

([Triple A](https://www.tripleatrailers.com/product/6x10-single-axle-concession-trailer-sink-package-electrical-finished-interior/)).

The **Advanced Concession 6x10 hot dog package costs $23,672**, but much of that price pays for a hood, a 40-lb fryer and a griddle that a steam-only menu does not use ([Advanced Concession Trailers](https://www.advancedconcessiontrailers.com/hot-dog-trailer-6-x-10/)). **Avoid China-made mini trailers ($2,200–$5,000).** They ship with **2-compartment sinks, which fail Texas rules**, 25 L tanks and 201-grade stainless steel, and their listings do not mention a DOT/VIN ([ETO Food Carts](https://www.etofoodcarts.com/food-carts/mini-food-trailers-for-sale.html)). ### Power plan: battery bank instead of a gas generator

**Why batteries fit Kyle.** Kyle's food truck court ordinance bans generators except in emergencies, "not to exceed two hours within any given six hour period" ([Kyle code](https://ecode360.com/45534712)). Hays County requires an extra extinguisher only when a generator is used. A battery bank has no fuel, no refueling distance rules, and runs nearly silent.

**Sizing (engineering estimate, not manufacturer data).**
- Peak running load: steamer 1,300 W + steam table 1,500 W + prep rail 360 W + pump/lights 300 W = **3,460 W** (about 4,180 W for a moment when the fridge compressor starts). The inverter must sustain about **3.5 kW at 120 V**.
- Preheat (steamer and steam table at full draw ~1 hour): **~2.8 kWh**. Fill with hot water and preheat on commissary shore power to cut most of this.
- Service: heaters cycle on thermostats, so average draw is about **1.3–2.1 kW**.
- Daily use: about **8–11 kWh for a 4-hour service** and **10.4–15.4 kWh for 6 hours**, plus ~10% inverter loss and a 10–20% reserve.
- **Keep the A/C and electric water heater off the battery.** A/C adds ~5.4 kWh per 6-hour day. Carry hot water from the commissary or run the heater only on shore power.

**Recharging.** Delta Pro 3: ~7–8 hours overnight on a dedicated 20 A 120 V circuit, or ~3–4 hours on 240 V. Victron: its 70 A charger draws ~3.8 kW and needs a **30 A (TT-30) inlet** for a 4–5 hour refill, or can be capped to charge from a 20 A outlet in ~8–10 hours. **Get overnight-charging permission and the outlet amperage in your commissary agreement.**

**Solar (optional, year two).** A 6x10 roof fits about 800–1,200 W of panels, roughly 3–5 kWh on a sunny day **[ESTIMATE]**. That's 20–30% of a service day: helpful, not a replacement for overnight charging.

**Safety and code.**
- Stay under **20 kWh** total: that's the NFPA 855 lithium-ion threshold where energy-storage rules kick in. Ask the Fire Marshal before expanding past it.
- Prefer UL 9540 systems or UL 1973 batteries with UL 9540A test data.
- Mount batteries in a ventilated, shaded compartment away from steam and water, strapped against movement.
- Texas heat: most units discharge up to about 104–113°F and **charge only up to about 104°F**. A closed trailer in summer can exceed that, so ventilate the compartment and charge at night.
- No DSHS or Hays County rule specific to batteries was found **[VERIFY]**. Kyle's checklist says the trailer can't draw power from an adjacent building without written permission.

**Battery vs. gas, at a glance.** Battery: ~$6,200–$8,400 up front, no fuel, nearly silent, allowed at Kyle food truck courts, 3,000–4,000+ cycles. Honda EU7000is: $4,899–$4,999, 58 dB(A), daily fuel, no refueling within 20 ft, generators banned at Kyle food truck courts, extra extinguisher required.

## Phase 5: commissary and suppliers

## Conclusion

The cheapest permit path and the menu are tied together. Steam-heated commercial franks fit the $1,018 Type II license and the no-hood fire assumption. Each step toward cooking on the unit, from reheating onions on a burner up to a griddle, moves Aaron toward Type III fees, hood questions and higher power load. Doing the onion reheat at the commissary and carrying the onions hot, or dropping caramelized onions until the business is proven, keeps the trailer in the simplest category. Phone calls 1 and 2 are therefore worth more than any equipment comparison.

The state takeover also helps a solo operator in Kyle. Kyle can no longer ban a DSHS-licensed vendor or cap vending hours, so the remaining local barriers are site-specific: a landlord's consent letter, a site plan and restroom access. Those are exactly what a Lowe's or Home Depot store relationship can provide. The weakest-sourced parts of this plan are local: Kyle's vendor fee, the current text of Ordinance 629, and whether the 2021 fire checklist still applies. Those items deserve the most verification effort.
